The Gospel in the Old Testament

Joshua M. Haub · June 29, 2026

There are so many amazing pictures of Jesus and the Gospel in the Old Testament. Jesus Himself taught the disciples about many of these connections (cf. Luke 24:44–47). One of these pictures is 2 Kings 6:24–7:20, especially 7:3–18.

Let me retell this story: Samaria is under siege by Aram. The Arameans have surrounded Samaria and the people inside the city are dying from starvation. Elisha, the man of God, pronounces a message: “Tomorrow, food will be cheap.” An amazing statement considering people were eating their own children and buying animals’ dung! Well, there were 4 leprous men in the gate of Samaria who decide that since they were doomed to die of starvation, they might as well see if they could get some food from the Arameans. The Arameans could kill them, but they were going to die anyway!

So, they went out to the camp. But when they got there, there was no one. Where did everyone go? Where did the enemies of God’s people flee to? God, in the night, had scared the army by creating a noise that sounded like an army coming, so the Arameans thought they were about to be attacked, and fled. And in their fleeing, they left all their stuff behind.

When the four leprous men saw all the Arameans’ stuff lying unprotected, they rush to take as much stuff as they possibly could! Here’s what happened:

“When these lepers came to the outskirts of the camp, they entered one tent and ate and drank, and carried from there silver and gold and clothes, and went and hid them; and they returned and entered another tent and carried from there also, and went and hid them. Then they said to one another, ‘We are not doing right. This day is a day of good news, but we are keeping silent; if we wait until morning light, punishment will overtake us. Now therefore come, let us go and tell the king’s household.’” (2 Kings 7:8–9, NASB95)

The lepers realized that this was not news they should keep to themselves—they needed to go and share it with the rest of the dying people of Samaria. They go. At first the king does not believe them, but after some investigation (“send and see,” 7:13), the news is accepted and those in the city rush to the spoil. God’s message through Elisha came true.

“So the people went out and plundered the camp of the Arameans. Then a measure of fine flour was sold for a shekel and two measures of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the Lord.” (2 Kings 7:16, NASB95)

I hope you can see some of the images of the Gospel in this story. Let me show you just a few:

Elisha. There is perhaps no greater foreshadower of Jesus than Elisha. His name means “God is Salvation.” Jesus’ name in Hebrew is “Joshua,” which means “YHWH is Salvation.” Elisha’s predecessor was Elijah. John the Baptist is called Elijah in Mal 4:5–6, Matt 17:10–13, and Luke 1:17. Both Elisha and Jesus bring a message of hope to people: Salvation! The note also contains connections between Elijah and John the Baptist; Elijah and Jesus.

Lepers. These four leprous men are incredible images of us. Leprosy was one of the worst diseases. If you were a leper, you were cast out of the camp of God’s people. It made you unclean. That’s exactly what sin has done to us: caused us to be spiritually sick, diseased, and cast out of God’s camp. These men were starving to death—they were dying. Sin kills us too. Everyone lost in sin is spiritually dead. However, they found something. At first, they were enjoying it themselves, but then they realized they needed to go and share the “good news” (7:9) to all the other dying people. The Septuagint (the Greek version of the Old Testament) uses the word euangelia (“good news”). In the New Testament, Jesus is said to proclaim the euangelion (“gospel”; “good news”) in Matt 4:23. It is the same word. The lepers realized they had good news (Gospel) to share with others who were dying too. They had to share it! That is what we do: share the Gospel with the spiritually dead.

Send and See. When the message about the good news of the Arameans fleeing came to the king of Samaria, he was skeptical at first. Maybe the Arameans were playing a trick on those in Samaria! One servant, however, told the king he should send some men to check it out and see if the Arameans are playing a trick or not. The servant said: “So let us send and see” (7:13). There was skepticism about the news about Jesus in the NT too, specifically Nathanael when Philip told him that Jesus was the one Moses and the Prophets wrote about. Nathanael questions, “Can anything good come out of Nazareth?” Philip responded, “Come and see” (John 1:46). And once Nathanael learned that Philip was right, he believed in Jesus and followed Him. Once the king knew the truth, his people enjoyed the spoils of the Arameans.

I am sure there are more connections to be made between 2 Kings 6–7 and the Gospel, but I hope these few help you see that the Gospel Jesus brought to us was the one God was teaching His people about all throughout the Old Testament. Keep looking for Jesus in the Old Testament. The Gospel is in the Old Testament.

To Him be the Glory.
